# Generic and exponential series of small graphs (Section 6.10)

These scripts produce the data of Remarks 6.21 and 6.23, the certificate
of Proposition 6.26 (Conjecture 6.25 on graphs with at most seven
vertices), and the unimodality count in the Outlook. Unlike the rest of the code, they compute
ranks **modulo a prime**. Reduction modulo a prime can only lower the rank
of a matrix, so every cumulative dimension computed here is a lower bound
for the corresponding dimension over the rationals; the individual Hilbert
coefficients need not be. These values are evidence, with one exception:
where a modular cumulative rank attains a proved upper bound, equality over
Q follows (`low_certificate.py`, below). The exact checks of
Proposition 6.20 and of the rational points of Remark 6.21 are in
`../../search/exp_small.py`.

Requirements: Python 3, NumPy; networkx for `bonus_low.py`,
`low_certificate.py` and `summary.py` (graph atlas and invariants). Set
`OMP_NUM_THREADS=1` when running with several processes.

| Script | What it does | Output |
|---|---|---|
| `gz.py` | the subalgebra generated by the `X_v`, its word filtration and Hilbert function modulo a prime (default 1048573); dense linear algebra with exact float64 products (guarded by an assertion) | |
| `graphs_list.py` | the 41 graphs: all connected simple graphs with 2 to 5 vertices, the multigraphs of the tables in `../../tables/`, both graphs of [NSh, Example 2], the doubled triangle and E2, E3, E4 | |
| `phase1.py` | generic series (three random parameters, which agree) and the exponential for all 41 graphs | `results/phase1.{txt,json,log}` |
| `scan.py p NAMES` | complete scan of the normalized parameter space over GF(p) (`ALL3`, `ALL4` select the graphs with maximum degree 3 or 4) | `results/scan_p{p}_*.json`, `results/scan*.log` |
| `classify.py p` | assigns every exceptional point of the scans to the loci A, B, C, Q, L, W, E, K of Remark 6.21; no point is left over for p = 61 or 101 | `results/classify_p{p}.{txt,json}` |
| `fitcurves.py` | fits weighted-homogeneous curves to the exceptional point sets (how the loci were found) | |
| `six.py 6 15` | generic and exponential series of all 112 connected simple graphs with six vertices (Remark 6.23) | `results/six_6.log`, `results/six_6_15.json` |
| `lowdeg.py`, `bonus_low.py` | h_0, …, h_4 (h_0, …, h_3 for seven vertices) for generic f and the exponential, all connected simple graphs with 4 to 7 vertices | `results/bonus_low.json` |

Commands (from this directory; `../../run_all.sh generic_exp` runs them all;
the times are those of the recorded run, on four cores):

```
python3 phase1.py
python3 scan.py 101 ALL3; python3 scan.py 101 ALL4      # about 25 min with 4 processes
python3 scan.py 61 ALL3 ALL4                             # about 11 min
python3 classify.py 101; python3 classify.py 61
python3 six.py 6 15                                      # about 15 min, mostly K6
python3 bonus_low.py                                     # about 8 min
python3 low_certificate.py --jobs 4                      # about 4 min
python3 summary.py
```

The scans write into `results/`; `scan.py` reads `results/phase1.json` for
the generic series.

## Proposition 6.26: Conjecture 6.25 for n ≤ 7

Let F_d be the span of the products of at most d of the Y_v. Over Q,
Proposition 6.24 gives dim F_1 ≤ U_1 = n + 1 and the bounds (6.20) on h_2
and h_3, hence

    dim F_2 ≤ U_2 = U_1 + C(n+1, 2) − p(G) − ε_2,
    dim F_3 ≤ U_3 = U_2 + C(n+2, 3) − d_2(G) − e_22(G) − ε_3.

For an integer parameter (or a rational one with denominators prime to p),
the rank of the same matrix modulo p is at most its rank over Q, as in
(6.21). If the modular cumulative ranks attain U_1 and U_2 (part (i)), or
U_1 and U_3 (part (ii)), equality holds over Q at that parameter, and since
the rank at a generic parameter is at least the rank at any special one and
at most the bound, equality holds for generic f as well.

`low_certificate.py` computes the cumulative ranks modulo p = 1048573 for
every connected simple graph with 4 to 7 vertices that is not a tree (970
graphs from the networkx graph atlas), at a fixed random integer parameter
and at the exponential, and compares them with U_1, U_2, U_3:

- part (i), the 968 graphs other than C_4 and the triangle with a pendant
  edge: certified for all 968 at the integer parameter, hence for generic f,
  and for all 968 at the exponential;
- part (ii), the 557 bridgeless graphs with cyclomatic number at least 3
  other than K_4: certified for all 557 at the integer parameter, and for 555
  at the exponential. For the two remaining graphs (atlas G150 and G152, both
  with six vertices and eight edges) the modular rank of F_3 at the
  exponential is one below U_3. A modular rank below the bound decides
  nothing over Q, so the script recomputes these two Hilbert functions
  exactly, in rational arithmetic (`../../search/hilbert_direct.py`): over
  Q, dim F_3 = U_3 − 1 as well (h_3 = 51 instead of 52 for G150, 52 instead
  of 53 for G152), so the exponential does not attain the cubic bound on
  these two graphs.

`results/low_certificate.json` records, for each graph, its edges, the
invariants p(G), d_2(G), e_22(G), Δ(G), the parameter used, the bounds
U_1, U_2, U_3 and the computed ranks.
